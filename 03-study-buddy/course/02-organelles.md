# Lecture 2: Organelles and the endomembrane system

*Introductory Cell Biology (BIO 110), synthetic course notes written for this demo.*

## Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells

Prokaryotes (bacteria and archaea) have no nucleus: their single circular chromosome sits in a
region called the nucleoid. Eukaryotic cells (animals, plants, fungi, protists) keep their DNA
in a membrane-bound **nucleus** and divide their interior into membrane-bound organelles. Both
kinds of cell have ribosomes, cytoplasm and a plasma membrane.

## The nucleus

The nucleus is surrounded by a double membrane, the **nuclear envelope**, perforated by nuclear
pores that let mRNA out and proteins in. Inside, the **nucleolus** is where ribosomal RNA is
made and ribosome subunits are assembled. It is not surrounded by a membrane.

## Ribosomes

Ribosomes build proteins. They are made of rRNA and protein and have no membrane. **Free
ribosomes** float in the cytosol and make proteins used inside the cytosol. **Bound
ribosomes** sit on the rough ER and make proteins that will be secreted, placed in the plasma
membrane or sent to lysosomes.

## The endomembrane system

- **Rough endoplasmic reticulum (rough ER):** studded with ribosomes, so it looks rough under the
  electron microscope. It makes and folds proteins destined for secretion or for membranes.
  Plasma cells, which secrete antibodies, are packed with rough ER.
- **Smooth endoplasmic reticulum (smooth ER):** has no ribosomes. It makes lipids, including
  phospholipids and steroid hormones, detoxifies drugs and alcohol (liver cells have a lot of
  it), and stores calcium ions (in muscle cells it is called the sarcoplasmic reticulum).
- **Golgi apparatus:** a stack of flattened sacs (cisternae) that receives vesicles from the ER on
  its *cis* face, modifies proteins (for example by adding sugar groups), sorts them and ships
  them from its *trans* face to their destinations. Think of it as the cell's post office.
- **Lysosomes:** vesicles of digestive enzymes that work best at an acidic pH of about 5. They
  break down worn-out organelles (autophagy) and material brought in by phagocytosis.

## Energy organelles

**Mitochondria** carry out cellular respiration and make most of the cell's ATP. They have a
smooth outer membrane and a folded inner membrane; the folds are called **cristae** and the
fluid inside is the **matrix**. **Chloroplasts**, found in plants and algae, carry out
photosynthesis; their stacked thylakoid membranes (grana) hold chlorophyll and the fluid
around them is the stroma.

Both mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own circular DNA and their own ribosomes and
divide on their own. This is the evidence for the **endosymbiotic theory**: they descend from
free-living prokaryotes that were engulfed by an ancestral cell.

## Plant versus animal cells

Plant cells have a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole that stores
water and keeps the cell turgid. Animal cells have none of these but have centrioles and
usually more lysosomes.

## Common exam traps

- Rough ER makes proteins; smooth ER makes lipids and detoxifies. Students often swap them.
- The nucleolus makes ribosome parts; ribosomes themselves make proteins.
- The Golgi modifies and ships proteins; it does not make them.
