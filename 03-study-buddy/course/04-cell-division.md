# Lecture 4: Cell division — mitosis and meiosis

## The cell cycle

A dividing cell spends most of its life in **interphase**: G1 (growth), S (DNA synthesis, when
each chromosome is copied into two identical **sister chromatids** joined at the centromere)
and G2 (more growth, preparation for division). Then comes the mitotic phase. Checkpoints at
G1, G2 and during metaphase stop a damaged cell from dividing; cancer is, at heart, a failure of
these checkpoints.

## Mitosis: one division, two identical cells

Mitosis divides the nucleus of a body (somatic) cell. It is used for growth, repair and asexual
reproduction. The stages are:

1. **Prophase:** chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes; the spindle starts to form.
2. **Metaphase:** chromosomes line up single file along the middle of the cell, the metaphase
   plate.
3. **Anaphase:** the **sister chromatids** separate and are pulled to opposite poles.
4. **Telophase:** nuclear envelopes re-form around the two sets of chromosomes.

**Cytokinesis** then splits the cytoplasm: a cleavage furrow pinches animal cells in two, while
plant cells build a cell plate. The result of mitosis is **two genetically identical diploid
(2n) daughter cells**. A human body cell has 46 chromosomes before and each daughter has 46
after.

## Meiosis: two divisions, four different cells

Meiosis makes gametes (sperm and eggs) and halves the chromosome number, so that fertilisation
restores it. It has **two rounds of division after one round of DNA copying**.

- **Meiosis I** separates **homologous chromosomes** (the matching maternal and paternal copies).
  In **prophase I** homologues pair up as tetrads and swap segments in **crossing over**, at
  points called chiasmata. In metaphase I the pairs line up two by two, and their orientation is
  random (**independent assortment**). At the end there are two haploid cells.
- **Meiosis II** looks like mitosis: the **sister chromatids** separate.

The result is **four genetically different haploid (n) cells**. In humans each has 23
chromosomes.

## Side by side

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Divisions | 1 | 2 |
| Daughter cells | 2 | 4 |
| Chromosome number | diploid (2n), same as parent | haploid (n), half the parent |
| Genetically | identical to parent | all different |
| Crossing over | no | yes, in prophase I |
| What separates | sister chromatids | homologues (I), then sister chromatids (II) |
| Used for | growth, repair | making gametes |

Errors in separation are called **nondisjunction**; a gamete with an extra copy of chromosome 21
can lead to Down syndrome (trisomy 21).

## Common exam traps

- Crossing over happens in prophase **I** of meiosis, never in mitosis.
- Homologous chromosomes separate in meiosis I; sister chromatids separate in mitosis and in
  meiosis II.
- Mitosis keeps the chromosome number; meiosis halves it.
