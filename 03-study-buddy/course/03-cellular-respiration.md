# Lecture 3: Cellular respiration

*Introductory Cell Biology (BIO 110), synthetic course notes written for this demo.*

Cellular respiration releases the energy stored in glucose and captures it as ATP. The overall
equation is: glucose + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O + energy (ATP and heat). It runs in four stages.

## 1. Glycolysis (cytoplasm)

Glycolysis happens in the **cytoplasm**, not in the mitochondrion, and it does not need oxygen.
One glucose (6 carbons) is split into **two molecules of pyruvate** (3 carbons each). The cell
spends 2 ATP and makes 4, for a **net gain of 2 ATP**, plus 2 NADH.

## 2. Pyruvate oxidation (mitochondrial matrix)

Each pyruvate is carried into the mitochondrion, loses one carbon as CO₂ and becomes
**acetyl-CoA**, producing one NADH per pyruvate.

## 3. The Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix)

Also called the citric acid cycle. Acetyl-CoA joins a 4-carbon molecule (oxaloacetate) to make
citrate, which is broken down step by step, releasing CO₂. Per glucose (two turns of the
cycle) the Krebs cycle yields 2 ATP, 6 NADH and 2 FADH₂. Most of the CO₂ you breathe out is
made here.

## 4. Oxidative phosphorylation (inner mitochondrial membrane)

NADH and FADH₂ hand their electrons to the **electron transport chain**, a series of proteins
in the **inner mitochondrial membrane**. As electrons pass along the chain, protons (H⁺) are
pumped from the matrix into the intermembrane space, building a gradient. Protons flow back
through **ATP synthase**, which uses that flow to make ATP; this coupling is called
**chemiosmosis**. **Oxygen is the final electron acceptor**: it takes the spent electrons and
combines with protons to form water. Without oxygen the chain backs up and stops.

This stage makes by far the most ATP: about 26–28 of the roughly **30–32 ATP** produced per
glucose.

## When there is no oxygen: fermentation

Without oxygen, cells fall back on glycolysis alone and use fermentation to regenerate the NAD⁺
glycolysis needs. **Lactic acid fermentation** (in muscle cells during hard exercise, and in the
bacteria that make yoghurt) turns pyruvate into lactate. **Alcoholic fermentation** (in yeast)
turns pyruvate into ethanol and CO₂, which is why bread rises. Fermentation yields only the
2 ATP from glycolysis.

## Respiration and photosynthesis

Photosynthesis in chloroplasts builds glucose from CO₂ and water using light energy and
releases O₂; respiration in mitochondria breaks glucose down using O₂ and releases CO₂. Plants
do both. Respiration is not the same as breathing: breathing moves air, respiration is the
chemistry inside cells.

## Common exam traps

- Glycolysis is in the cytoplasm; the Krebs cycle is in the matrix; the electron transport chain
  is in the inner membrane.
- Oxygen's job is to accept electrons at the very end of the chain, not to make CO₂.
- The Krebs cycle itself makes little ATP directly; its main products are NADH and FADH₂.
