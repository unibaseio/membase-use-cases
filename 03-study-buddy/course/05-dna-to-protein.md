# Lecture 5: From DNA to protein

*Introductory Cell Biology (BIO 110), synthetic course notes written for this demo.*

## DNA structure

DNA is a double helix of two antiparallel strands. Each nucleotide has a deoxyribose sugar, a
phosphate and one of four bases. Bases pair by hydrogen bonds: **adenine with thymine** (two
bonds) and **guanine with cytosine** (three bonds). RNA differs in three ways: it uses ribose,
it is usually single-stranded, and it has **uracil instead of thymine**.

## DNA replication (S phase)

Replication is **semi-conservative**: each new double helix keeps one old strand and gains one
new strand.

- **Helicase** unzips the double helix by breaking the hydrogen bonds between bases, opening a
  replication fork.
- **Primase** lays down a short RNA primer, because DNA polymerase cannot start a strand from
  nothing.
- **DNA polymerase** adds nucleotides to the new strand, and only in the **5′ → 3′ direction**.
  It also proofreads.
- The **leading strand** is built continuously toward the fork. The **lagging strand** is built
  away from the fork in short pieces called **Okazaki fragments**.
- **DNA ligase** seals the gaps between Okazaki fragments.

## The central dogma

Information flows **DNA → RNA → protein**. A gene is a stretch of DNA that codes for a protein
(or a functional RNA).

## Transcription: DNA to mRNA (in the nucleus)

**Transcription** copies a gene into messenger RNA. **RNA polymerase** binds the **promoter**,
unwinds the DNA and builds an mRNA strand complementary to the template strand. In eukaryotes
this happens in the **nucleus**, and the pre-mRNA is then processed: a 5′ cap and a poly-A tail
are added, and **introns are spliced out** so that only the **exons** remain. The mature mRNA
leaves through a nuclear pore.

## Translation: mRNA to protein (at the ribosome)

**Translation** reads the mRNA and builds a chain of amino acids. It happens at a **ribosome in
the cytoplasm** (free or on the rough ER). The mRNA is read in three-base **codons**. Each
**transfer RNA (tRNA)** carries one amino acid and has an **anticodon** that pairs with a
matching codon. The start codon **AUG** codes for methionine; the stop codons UAA, UAG and UGA
code for no amino acid and end the chain. There are 64 codons for 20 amino acids, so most amino
acids have more than one codon (the code is redundant).

## Mutations

A **point mutation** changes one base. A **silent** mutation changes a codon into another for the
same amino acid; a **missense** mutation changes the amino acid (sickle-cell anaemia is one); a
**nonsense** mutation creates an early stop codon. Inserting or deleting a base causes a
**frameshift**, which scrambles every codon after it.

## Common exam traps

- Transcription makes RNA from DNA in the nucleus; translation makes protein from mRNA at the
  ribosome. The names are easy to swap.
- Helicase unzips; DNA polymerase builds. Primase and ligase are the helpers at either end.
- tRNA carries the anticodon; mRNA carries the codon.
