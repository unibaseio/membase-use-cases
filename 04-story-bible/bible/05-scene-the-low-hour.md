# Scene 2: The Low Hour

The third bell stops. The channels between the ribs begin to drain.

Ilse stands at her horn window on the third tier and watches the water leave. It goes the way it
always goes: all at once, with a sound like a breath drawn in through teeth. The Rib Bridges
stand bare above the mud. Nobody is on them. Nobody is ever on them.

She drinks her kelp tea from the dented tin cup. It is bitter, and that is the point.

On the map table the brown ink of the morning's chart is still wet. She has drawn the drain
lines of the Low Hour forty times. They have never once matched the drain lines of the night
Corin drowned.

Somewhere below, Pell is counting backwards. Ilse cannot hear the numbers, only the rhythm.

The water comes back. It always comes back. It fills the channels to the third rib in the time
it takes to say a name, and the horn window glows grey, and the compass in her pocket turns a
quarter-turn toward the Spine Chapel, as if something up there had shifted in its sleep.
